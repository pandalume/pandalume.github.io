Cypher Vault

  • Your vault is encrypted on your device with your master password. If you sign in, only the encrypted vault is backed up, so the stored copy cannot be read by us or anyone else without your master password. We cannot recover your vault if you lose both your master password and your recovery key.
  • Sign-in: backup, sync and updates use Google sign-in. We receive your email address and an account ID. Signing in is optional; without it the vault stays only on your device.
  • Updates: while you are signed in, Cypher Vault checks once a day (and when you tap Check for updates) whether a newer version is available to your account. The check sends your sign-in, the app's name and your platform (Android or Windows), never your vault. When you tap Update, the new version is downloaded from GitHub and checked against its published fingerprint before it is installed.
  • Website icons: off unless you turn on Site icons. Then, to show a site's icon next to an entry, Cypher Vault asks Google's icon service for that website's icon, which tells Google the website's address (not your username or password).
  • Breached-password check: only when you run it. Cypher Vault sends only the first 5 characters of a one-way fingerprint (SHA-1 hash) of the password to the Have I Been Pwned service. The password itself never leaves your device.
  • Browser extension: talks only to the Cypher Vault app on the same computer. It does not send your data anywhere else. If you save a new login while the app is locked, the app keeps it encrypted on your computer until you unlock. If the app is closed, the extension keeps it in the browser, encrypted so that only your Cypher Vault app can open it, and hands it over the next time the app runs.

Services Cypher Vault uses

  • Google: sign-in, and website icons (if turned on).
  • Supabase (servers in Singapore): accounts, the encrypted vault backup, and the update check.
  • GitHub: hosts the update files you download when you tap Update.
  • Have I Been Pwned (Pwned Passwords): breached-password checks, as described above.
